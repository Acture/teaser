# TACO trademark policy

The source code and documentation are licensed separately under the GNU Affero
General Public License. That copyright license does not grant permission to use the
TACO name, logo, or other project branding in a way that suggests an unofficial
product is the official TACO product.

## Uses that do not require permission

- truthfully referring to the TACO project;
- stating that software is based on, compatible with, or derived from TACO while
  clearly identifying it as unofficial and unendorsed;
- linking to the official repository;
- distributing an unmodified official TACO source or build while preserving its
  legal notices and clearly identifying its source;
- using the name in commentary, education, research, reviews, or community groups
  where no endorsement is implied.

## Uses that require permission

- distributing modified software under the name “TACO” or with the official logo;
- naming a fork, product, service, store listing, domain, or social account in a
  confusingly similar way;
- implying that the TACO maintainers sponsor, certify, or endorse a third-party
  product or service.

Modified distributions should use a distinct name and remove the official logo.
They may say, for example, “Based on TACO,” provided that the statement is accurate
and does not imply endorsement. They must still satisfy the AGPL and preserve
applicable copyright and third-party notices.

This policy protects source identification; it does not add an attribution or
advertising condition to the AGPL or limit rights granted by that license to the
code itself. Questions or permission requests should be opened through the official
repository until a dedicated contact address is published.

The TACO name is short and may overlap with existing marks. This policy records the
project's intended usage boundary; formal registration or enforcement should follow
a jurisdiction-specific trademark search and legal review.
