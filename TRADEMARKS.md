# Teaser trademark policy

The source code and documentation are licensed separately under the GNU Affero
General Public License. That copyright license does not grant permission to use the
Teaser name, logo, or other project branding in a way that suggests an unofficial
product is the official Teaser product.

## Uses that do not require permission

- truthfully referring to the Teaser project;
- stating that software is based on, compatible with, or derived from Teaser while
  clearly identifying it as unofficial and unendorsed;
- linking to the official repository;
- distributing an unmodified official Teaser source or build while preserving its
  legal notices and clearly identifying its source;
- using the name in commentary, education, research, reviews, or community groups
  where no endorsement is implied.

## Uses that require permission

- distributing modified software under the name “Teaser” or with the official logo;
- naming a fork, product, service, store listing, domain, or social account in a
  confusingly similar way;
- implying that the Teaser maintainers sponsor, certify, or endorse a third-party
  product or service.

Modified distributions should use a distinct name and remove the official logo.
They may say, for example, “Based on Teaser,” provided that the statement is
accurate and does not imply endorsement. They must still satisfy the AGPL and
preserve applicable copyright and third-party notices.

This policy protects source identification; it does not add an attribution or
advertising condition to the AGPL or limit rights granted by that license to the
code itself. Questions or permission requests should be opened through the official
repository until a dedicated contact address is published.

The Teaser name may overlap with existing marks. This policy records the project's
intended usage boundary; it does not claim registration or legal clearance. Formal
registration or enforcement should follow a jurisdiction-specific trademark search
and legal review.
